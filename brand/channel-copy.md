## Channel trailer — 60 seconds

The highest-leverage copy on a new channel: it plays to people who have
never heard of you and are deciding in about four seconds. Write it to
sell the *premise*, never the episode list.

**[0:00–0:08]**
> There's a steel box that made everything in your house cheaper.
> There's a reason half the world drives on the left.
> Nobody chose either one because it was better.

**[0:08–0:20]**
> This is Plainsight.
>
> Every episode, one ordinary object — and the argument, accident or
> agreement that made it the way it is.

**[0:20–0:40]**
> Not trivia. Not fifteen facts in a row.
>
> One thing, explained properly, until the moment it stops looking
> obvious — and then the part nobody mentions, which is usually who paid
> for it.

**[0:40–0:55]**
> The pattern shows up everywhere once you've seen it. Screw threads.
> Railway gauges. The plug on your wall that doesn't fit anywhere else
> on Earth.
>
> Most things aren't the way they are because they're right. They're
> that way because changing costs too much.

**[0:55–1:00]**
> New episode every other week.
> Have a look around.

**Production:** reuse finished shots from Episodes 01 and 02 — do not
shoot this separately. Cut it *after* two episodes exist, so it's made of
real footage. A trailer promising a style you haven't produced yet is the
most common way new channels misrepresent themselves.

## Channel sections (homepage layout)

Order matters — this is the browse path for a first-time visitor.

1. **Trailer** (for new visitors) / **latest upload** (for subscribers)
2. **Start here** — a hand-picked playlist, best episode first, *not*
   chronological. Nobody's first episode is their best one.
3. **Shorts**
4. **All episodes** — newest first

Keep it to four. A new channel with nine sections looks padded, because
it is.

## Per-episode description template

First two lines are all that show before "…more". Put the hook there and
nothing else — no socials, no "don't forget to subscribe."

```
[One-sentence restatement of the counterintuitive claim.]
[One sentence on what the episode actually answers.]

Chapters:
0:00 [Hook]
...

Sources and corrections:
[Numbered list. Link primary sources, not aggregators.]

Corrections are pinned in the comments and noted here.

Plainsight — the hidden logic of ordinary things.
New episode every other week.
```

**Publish your sources.** In explainer content it's the cheapest possible
trust signal, and it's the thing that protects you the first time a
figure turns out to be wrong. Both episode scripts ship with a
fact-check list for exactly this reason — the widely repeated shipping
cost figures and the sword-arm origin story are the two most likely to
bite.

## Tone rules

Consistency across copy matters more than any single line.

- **Never** "Hey guys," "welcome back," "in today's video," "let's dive in."
- No "mind-blowing," "insane," "you won't believe."
- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Contractions, always. Write how you speak.
- Admit uncertainty out loud — "the honest answer is the record is thin"
  is a *feature* of this channel, not a weakness.
- Never open a video with the channel name. The hook comes first, the
  brand comes at 6:40.
