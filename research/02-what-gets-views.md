# What Kind of Educational Video Actually Gets Views (2026 data)

Research pass: September 2026. Sources at the bottom.

## Headline numbers

| Finding | Number | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form view growth YoY | **+76%** | Long-form is still the growth engine |
| Long-form avg. view duration | **-37%** | But people watch less of each one |
| Shorts view growth YoY | **+127%** | Shorts are the discovery channel |
| Shorts view duration vs long | **-67%** | And they build almost no depth |
| Shorts engagement vs long | **-63%** | Shorts do not convert to community |
| Watch-time share, long vs short | **>10x** | Long-form owns total minutes watched |
| Viewers who drop in first 3 sec | **50-60%** | The hook is more than half the job |
| Good retention, sub-5-min video | **50-70%** | Your benchmark to beat |

Read those together and the strategy writes itself: **Shorts for reach and
for cheap hook testing, long-form for authority and watch time.** Neither
alone is a channel.

## The format that consistently wins in education

Across the top channels, the reliable shape is the **counterintuitive
explainer**: take one ordinary thing the viewer has never questioned,
show them it is stranger than they assumed, then resolve it cleanly.

It works because it satisfies both of the reasons people click on
educational content: to *solve a problem* and to *feel more capable*.

Concrete format ranking for a new channel:

1. **"Why does X work this way?"** — counterintuitive, single-object focus.
   Best ratio of effort to payoff. Start here.
2. **"What actually happens when X?"** — process/mechanism reveal. Very
   animation-friendly.
3. **"The hidden history of X"** — narrative pull, low visual demand,
   cheap to produce. The riser channels lean on this.
4. **"X, but explained properly"** — corrects a popular misconception.
   High comment volume, which the algorithm likes.
5. **Listicles / "top 10"** — high volume, low loyalty. Avoid early.

## Titles

- Front-load the keyword; put the emotional hook second, the promise last.
- **Question marks perform strongly specifically in Education, Business,
  and Science & Tech.** Use them here; they underperform elsewhere.
- **Statistics hooks outperform for educational content.**
- Keep the title short enough that the curiosity gap *cannot be resolved
  without watching.* A title that answers itself has no reason to be
  clicked.
- Name the specific viewer rather than being vague.

## Hooks: the first three seconds

Over half your audience leaves before second four, so the opening is not a
place for a logo, a greeting, or "in today's video." The eight hook
families that work: question, bold claim, statistic, story, controversy,
mystery/curiosity-gap, challenge, direct address.

For this channel the highest-yield three:

- **Bold claim** — state something that sounds wrong, then earn it.
- **Statistic** — one number the viewer cannot believe.
- **Mystery** — open a loop only the video can close.

Rules: no intro animation before the hook; speak the first word within
half a second of frame one; put motion on screen immediately; and make the
first sentence a *complete* provocation, not a windup to one.

## Length

- Sub-5 minutes must hold 50-70% retention to be judged good.
- Shorts can stretch to 35-45 seconds *if the payoff is explicit*.
- Educational viewers skip to the section they came for. Chapter your
  long-form videos; do not fight the behaviour.

## The plan this implies

- **Long-form:** one 6-9 minute counterintuitive explainer every two weeks.
- **Shorts:** 2-3 per week, each one a hook test. Cheap to make, and the
  Short that overperforms tells you what the next long-form should be.
- Test the hook *before* you animate it. Animation is the expensive part;
  a failed hook wastes it.
